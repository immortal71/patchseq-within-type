## Author summary (in the manuscript, right after the abstract)

Neurons of different kinds fire in different ways, and they also make different sets of ion channel genes. A technique called Patch-seq lets researchers record a neuron's electrical behaviour and read its genes from the very same cell, and studies using it report that channel genes predict electrical behaviour well. I asked a narrower question: among neurons of the same kind, do differences in channel genes still predict differences in firing? Using public recordings from about 1,300 mouse neurons, I found that almost all of the predictive power disappears once each neuron's type is accounted for. Because weak effects are hard to measure in datasets of this size, I also estimated how large the hidden within-type relationship could be. For the best-predicted properties it is at most about a tenth of the variation, several times smaller than the headline figures, and for the cell's resting voltage I found none at all. This matters because channel genes are often used as a stand-in for a cell's electrical state. Within a cell type, that stand-in is weak.
